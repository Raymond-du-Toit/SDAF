# Contributing to SDAF

Thank you for helping to review, test and improve the Sustainable Digital Architecture Framework (SDAF).

SDAF is published openly so that practitioners, researchers, standards bodies, scheme owners, technology communities and other interested parties can evaluate it, apply it and contribute evidence for its continuing development.

This guide explains how to participate while preserving the integrity and traceability of the published framework.

## 1. Published versions and repository work

SDAF Version 1.0 is a fixed, citable publication preserved at:

<https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22899663>

Activity in this repository does not silently alter the published v1.0 release. Accepted corrections, clarifications and proposals will be recorded transparently and, where appropriate, incorporated into a future published version.

The DOI representing all published SDAF versions is:

<https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22899662>

## 2. Ways to contribute

Contributions may include:

- reporting an editorial or accessibility issue;
- identifying a normative ambiguity, inconsistency or conflict;
- asking an implementation or interpretation question;
- sharing implementation experience or pilot evidence;
- proposing a mapping to a standard, regulation, certification scheme or specialist methodology;
- contributing a worked example, template or interoperability pattern;
- identifying a measurement, lifecycle, circularity or assurance issue;
- proposing an improvement for a future version; or
- raising a documented concern about a public SDAF conformance claim.

Focused, evidence-based contributions are particularly valuable. You do not need to be a sustainability specialist or standards expert to participate.

## 3. Before opening an issue

Before submitting a new issue:

1. Check the published framework and relevant annexes.
2. Search existing open and closed issues for the same topic.
3. Identify the SDAF version and, where possible, the Part, section, requirement, table or figure concerned.
4. Separate observed facts from proposed interpretations or solutions.
5. Remove confidential, personal, proprietary or security-sensitive information.

Do not upload material that you do not have permission to share.

## 4. Issue categories

Choose the category that best matches the contribution.

### Editorial or accessibility correction

Use for typographical errors, broken links, formatting defects, inaccessible content or wording corrections that do not change methodological meaning.

Include:

- document version;
- page and section;
- current wording or presentation;
- proposed correction; and
- reason for the correction.

### Normative ambiguity or inconsistency

Use where a `SHALL`, `SHALL NOT`, `SHOULD`, `MAY`, applicability rule or conformance requirement may be unclear, conflicting or incomplete.

Include:

- exact requirement identifiers;
- the competing interpretations;
- the practical consequence of each interpretation;
- relevant evidence or external authority; and
- a proposed resolution, if available.

### Implementation question

Use when guidance is needed to apply SDAF to a real context.

Include:

- the decision or use case;
- relevant system and organizational boundaries;
- the SDAF phase or requirement involved;
- what has already been attempted; and
- the specific point requiring clarification.

Questions may be generalized before publication to protect confidential or proprietary information.

### Implementation experience or pilot evidence

Use to share observed results from applying SDAF or a defined subset of it.

Include, where possible:

- scope and context;
- SDAF version and requirements applied;
- participant roles and level of prior expertise;
- evidence sources and limitations;
- effort, data and tooling requirements;
- outcomes and decision effects;
- deviations or adaptations; and
- lessons for future implementation.

Do not present an implementation as validated, certified or representative beyond the evidence provided.

### Standards or methodology alignment

Use to propose or correct an alignment between SDAF and an external authority, standard, scheme or methodology.

Include:

- the external source and applicable version;
- the specific clauses or concepts compared;
- the nature of the relationship, such as complementary, overlapping, dependent or conflicting;
- identified gaps or differences in authority; and
- any licensing or quotation constraints.

An alignment proposal must not imply endorsement by the external organization.

### Worked example, template or tool

Use to propose reusable implementation material.

Include:

- intended audience and decision context;
- SDAF requirements supported;
- required inputs and expected outputs;
- assumptions and limitations;
- whether the material is illustrative or normative; and
- the proposed licence.

Examples should not embed confidential data or vendor claims that cannot be substantiated.

### Future-version proposal

Use for a substantive addition or change that may affect scope, requirements, terminology, decision logic, conformance or interoperability.

Include:

- the problem being addressed;
- affected requirements and downstream dependencies;
- proposed change;
- evidence and use cases;
- compatibility implications;
- potential risks or unintended consequences; and
- a suggested transition approach.

### Conformance concern

Use only for a specific, documentable concern about a public SDAF conformance claim.

Include:

- the public claim and its source;
- the SDAF version claimed;
- the declared scope, exclusions and conditions;
- the applicable Annex IV-C requirements;
- the available evidence; and
- the precise discrepancy requiring examination.

Describe verifiable facts and requirement gaps. Do not use issues to make allegations about motives, competence, ethics or legality. Do not publish confidential correspondence or personal data.

## 5. Pull requests

For simple editorial, accessibility or repository corrections, a pull request may be appropriate.

Before opening a pull request:

1. Link it to an existing issue or explain the problem clearly.
2. Keep the change focused on one topic.
3. Identify whether the change is editorial, informative or potentially normative.
4. Explain how the change was checked.
5. Avoid unrelated reformatting.

Substantive normative changes should normally begin as an issue. They require impact assessment and should not be merged merely because wording has been proposed.

## 6. Evaluation of contributions

Contributions may be evaluated against:

- consistency with SDAF purpose and principles;
- methodological and evidential integrity;
- clarity and reproducibility;
- effects on conformance and assurance;
- compatibility with existing requirements;
- implementation value and proportionality;
- accessibility and international applicability;
- relationship to external authorities; and
- strength and transparency of supporting evidence.

Possible outcomes include:

- accepted for repository correction;
- accepted as clarification or guidance;
- accepted for a future version backlog;
- referred for specialist or community review;
- deferred pending evidence or implementation experience; or
- declined with a recorded rationale.

Acceptance for discussion does not guarantee incorporation into a published version.

## 7. Normative change and versioning

Changes that affect obligations, applicability, conformance, decision classification, evidence requirements or assurance expectations require controlled review.

Such changes should:

1. identify affected requirements and dependencies;
2. document the rationale and supporting evidence;
3. assess compatibility and transition impacts;
4. receive appropriate technical review;
5. be recorded in the change history; and
6. be incorporated through a formally identified future release.

Published SDAF versions remain available and citable after later versions are released.

## 8. Contribution rights and licence

By submitting a contribution, you confirm that:

- you created the contribution or have the right to submit it;
- the contribution does not knowingly include material that cannot be shared under the proposed terms;
- you identify relevant third-party material and restrictions; and
- you agree that accepted documentation and framework contributions may be distributed under CC BY 4.0, unless a different licence is expressly agreed before acceptance.

Submitting an issue or contribution does not transfer ownership of unrelated pre-existing material.

## 9. Attribution and acknowledgement

Material contributions may be acknowledged in release notes, repository history or contributor records, as appropriate to their nature and significance.

Participation does not imply endorsement of SDAF by a contributor or the contributor's organization, and does not imply endorsement, certification or official status for the contributor by the SDAF project.

## 10. Conduct

Engage respectfully, focus on evidence and requirements, and allow room for good-faith disagreement.

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory conduct, intimidation, deliberate misrepresentation and disclosure of confidential or personal information are not acceptable.

Technical criticism is welcome. Criticism should address the framework, evidence, implementation or proposal—not the character or motives of participants.

## 11. Security and sensitive matters

Do not open a public issue containing:

- credentials or access information;
- exploitable security details;
- personal data;
- confidential commercial information;
- legally privileged communications; or
- information subject to contractual or regulatory restriction.

A private reporting route may be added as the project develops. Until then, do not submit sensitive information through the repository.

## 12. Questions

For non-sensitive questions, open a GitHub Issue using the most relevant category and clearly identify it as a question.

Thank you for helping make sustainability-informed digital architecture more rigorous, practical and widely usable.
