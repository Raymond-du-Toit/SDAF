## Versioning principles

- Published releases are fixed, independently citable records.
- Repository activity does not silently amend a published release.
- Editorial corrections, clarifications and normative changes are distinguished from one another.
- Material changes are documented with their rationale, scope and compatibility implications.
- Normative changes are introduced only through an explicitly identified future release.
- Earlier versions remain available after a later version is published.

## Change classifications

Changes are classified as follows:

- **Added** — new requirements, guidance, annexes, examples or supporting material.
- **Changed** — modifications to existing content or requirements.
- **Clarified** — interpretation or explanatory improvements that do not intentionally alter a requirement.
- **Corrected** — editorial, accessibility, formatting, citation or factual corrections.
- **Deprecated** — content retained temporarily but planned for removal or replacement.
- **Removed** — content no longer included.
- **Security** — changes addressing security-sensitive guidance or repository practices.

For changes affecting normative requirements, entries should identify the relevant Part, section or requirement and link to the supporting issue or decision record.

## [1.0] — 2026-09-22

**Initial public release**

DOI: <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22899663>

### Added

- Established the Sustainable Digital Architecture Framework as an open, technology-neutral methodology for sustainability-informed digital architecture decisions.
- Defined the five-phase assessment lifecycle: Orient, Baseline, Evaluate, Architect and Assure.
- Published the eight-Part SDAF architecture:
  - Part I — Foundations;
  - Part II — Lifecycle;
  - Part III — Measurement;
  - Part IV — Decision-Making;
  - Part V — Lifecycle and Circularity;
  - Part VI — Governance and Assurance;
  - Part VII — Enterprise Operationalization; and
  - Part VIII — Ecosystem Interoperability.
- Established common principles, terminology, applicability rules and normative conventions.
- Defined measurement requirements covering boundaries, functional comparison, methods, data quality, uncertainty, sensitivity, normalization, confidence and traceability.
- Established decision requirements covering evidence sufficiency, hard constraints, weighting, thresholds, sensitivity, stability, decision classes and controlled decision records.
- Integrated lifecycle impacts and circularity benefits while retaining separate accounting and controls against double counting.
- Defined governance, independent assurance and conformance requirements.
- Defined enterprise operating-model, capability and adoption requirements.
- Defined external evidence-exchange and interoperability requirements.
- Included normative and informative annexes, templates, quick rules, checklists, worked examples and alignment material.
- Established Annex IV-C as the public SDAF conformance model.
- Added IV-C.8 requirements for transparent public conformance statements.
- Published copyright, CC BY 4.0 licensing, adaptation, third-party-material and attribution provisions.
- Established the Zenodo record, version-specific DOI and all-versions DOI.

### Release status

- Version 1.0 is the initial stable public baseline.
- No external organization is implied to sponsor, endorse, certify or govern SDAF by virtue of this release.
- Subsequent review and implementation experience will inform documented future releases.

## Recording future changes

For each accepted change, add a concise entry under `[Unreleased]` that includes:

1. the change classification;
2. the affected Part, section or requirement;
3. a plain-language description;
4. the relevant issue or decision reference;
5. whether the change is editorial, informative or normative; and
6. any compatibility, implementation or transition implications.

When a new version is published:

1. move accepted entries from `[Unreleased]` into the new version section;
2. add the publication date and version-specific DOI;
3. summarize material compatibility implications;
4. retain the preceding release history; and
5. reset `[Unreleased]` for subsequent work.
